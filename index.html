Quick sorter

A calendar is more than an “appointment tool”

It is best for things that happen on a specific date/time. For things you only need to reconsider on a future date, set a trusted reminder so your brain does not have to keep remembering them in advance.

“Someday / Maybe” is not avoidance

The key is to put it into a system you will actually review regularly. Separating “not now” from “never” is itself a clear decision.

⑤ Act

Choose what makes sense right now.

Filter by context, time, energy, and priority.

① Context

Where am I? What tools do I have? What can actually be done in this environment?

② Available Time

How long until the next thing that must happen? If you only have ten minutes, do not choose a task that needs two hours.

③ Energy

Is my energy low, medium, or high? Creative work, communication, and routine tasks require different kinds of energy.

④ Relative Priority

Among the options that fit the first three conditions, which is most worthwhile right now? You do not need a permanently correct answer—just a grounded choice for this moment.

Three kinds of “work” are all valid

Do defined work: Execute from your Next Actions list.
Handle unexpected work: Unexpected things are part of life.
Define your own work: When something is unclear, pause and clarify it.

You cannot get everything done

The goal is not to make the number on your list reach zero. It is to know where unfinished things are, why they are not being done now, and when you will review them again.

Projects

More than one step? Make it a project.

Define the outcome, then the next action.

1

Ask: Why?

Why is this worth doing? What purpose does it serve? A clear purpose helps you filter options and create better ones.

2

Picture “Success”

If this goes well, what will reality look like? Define the outcome before jumping to “What do I need to do?”

3

Brain-Dump Everything

Generate ideas first, then filter them. Put the details, concerns, opportunities, and options that may affect the outcome on the table.

4

Organize the Structure

Organize ideas by components, sequence, importance, or milestones. Simple projects do not need complicated plans.

5

Define the Next Action

Ask, “What can I move forward right now?” If you can start today, define one visible action.

50,000+ Feet

A practical principle: When a “big question” feels overwhelming, start at the ground level and clarify the actions in front of you. When you find yourself constantly busy without knowing why, zoom out and check whether your projects, responsibilities, goals, and direction are aligned.
